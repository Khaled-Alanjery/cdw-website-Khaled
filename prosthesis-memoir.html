Matrix Memoir

Post-conflict environments carry a lineage of trauma; the transformation of destruction into a system of environmental and spatial regeneration is a testimony to what occurred and will occur. Conventional preservation seeks to restore damaged architecture to its original form, stabilizing history and minimizing visible traces of conflict. Such approaches risk neutralizing the political and spatial tensions of trauma by returning buildings to a state of apparent normalcy.

Prosthesis
for Memoir

The question that emerges is not simply how to rebuild such structures, but how spatial environments might acknowledge forces embedded within their remains.

Should trauma be reconstructed to a state of solitude, as if nothing occurred?

Preserved as a static ruin … or transformed into a space that engages the memory of destruction while addressing contemporary challenges?

Prosthetic Preservation, in this world, is not a technique for fixing buildings; it's the refusal to govern normalcy.
